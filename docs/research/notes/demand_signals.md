# Demand signals and user pain points for AI-assisted vocabulary capture and spaced-repetition apps (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: Reddit (r/Anki, r/languagelearning, etc.) could not be accessed. The search and fetch tools block reddit.com for this agent, so Reddit sentiment is missing and I did not try to work around the block. As a partial substitute I used Hacker News threads (via the Algolia API), Anki forums, Substack posts and academic papers. Below, "data" means measured numbers. "Anecdote" means individual opinions from commenters or blog authors.

## 1. What learners say about manual vs AI-generated cards; complaints about Anki and about AI cards

### Takeaway
Making cards by hand is the complaint that comes up most often. On HN people describe it as the time sink that makes them drop Anki. Anki's own maintainers admit that onboarding and UI polish are weak. The pushback against AI-generated cards follows the same lines every time: (a) subtle factual or translation errors, (b) weak handling of polysemy and context, and (c) "making the card is part of learning." The middle ground most often asked for is "human picks the word, AI fills in the card, human can edit." That is the bot's current design.

### Cited Findings
**Anki pain points**
- Data/official: In "Anki's Growing Up" (Feb 2, 2026), Damien Elmes said he is gradually handing Anki's business operations and open-source stewardship to AnkiHub (The AnKing and Andrew Sanchez). He cited burnout. He said "UI polish" had been hard to prioritize. The AnkiHub team named beginner onboarding and usability barriers as "low-hanging fruit" and plans to bring in professional design help. Commitments: Anki stays open source, no VC, "No changes planned to Anki's pricing." Some community replies feared paywalls ("slowly cannibalize the free stuff"). — [Anki Forums: Anki's Growing Up](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/ankis-growing-up/68610)
- Data: AnkiMobile (iOS) is a one-time purchase at $24.99. It ranked #1 Top Paid Education app in the US on Apr 20, 2026. AppGoblin does not publish a revenue estimate for it. — [Marlvel ranking](https://marlvel.ai/rankings/us/ios/education-paid/2026-04-20), [AppGoblin AnkiMobile](https://appgoblin.info/apps/373493387)
- Data: AnkiDroid has 10M+ installs on Google Play (checked Oct 2026). ankitects/anki has about 31.8k GitHub stars and ankidroid/Anki-Android about 11.9k. — [Google Play AnkiDroid](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ichi2.anki), [GitHub anki](https://github.com/ankitects/anki), [GitHub AnkiDroid](https://github.com/ankidroid/Anki-Android)
- Anecdote (HN, Feb 2025, "Show HN: Ricotta – Language Learning to Replace Anki", 58 points / 79 comments): commenters said Anki behaves inconsistently across web, Mac and iOS and that audio playback is unreliable. Others said hand-building decks takes so much time that they stop studying. Some asked for offline use and Anki export. Several were suspicious of a free product ("Am I the product?"). — [HN 42966942](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42966942)
- Data (medical students, n=89, 2025): 97.6% used pre-made third-party cards and almost none made their own. 75% "never or rarely kept up with daily reviews." 67.9% used Anki at least 5 days a week. — [PMC12662189](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12662189/)

**AI-generated card complaints**
- Anecdote with a measured error rate: in a 100-card biology deck generated by Claude, a blogger found 3 subtly wrong cards (≥3%), including a reversed signaling gradient. Conclusion: AI-made cards are too risky because the learner can't spot the error (Metacelsus, Jun 13, 2025). — [De Novo Substack](https://denovo.substack.com/p/ai-generated-anki-cards-are-not-to)
- Anecdote (HN Ricotta thread): AI-generated language cards got homonyms and polysemous words wrong because they lacked context. Tenses were mistranslated (a Spanish future rendered as English present). German nouns were missing articles. C1/C2 sentences were too easy. — [HN 42966942](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42966942)
- Anecdote (HN, Dec 2024, "Show HN: Anki AI Utils", 253 points / 43 comments): several commenters argued that creating cards is itself learning ("Creating cards is also learning... you'll be missing out"). Others asked whether AI tools are suitable for novices. — [HN 42534931](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42534931)
- Anecdote (HN, Nov 2025, "Show HN: Anki-LLM", 60 points / 23 comments): one user said LLM cards speed up language learning but need checking by a native speaker. Another said hand-curated decks feel more motivating. A third described a hybrid: create the card manually at the moment of discovery, then let the LLM add etymology and collocations. Others said cards need the source sentence as context. — [HN 45790443](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45790443)
- Data (qualitative, n=6, Feb 2026): students in the SmartFlash LLM flashcard study liked having less prep work but wanted outputs to be transparent and editable to keep "cognitive ownership." They treated the AI as a partner whose reasoning they could check, not as an autonomous tool. — [arXiv 2602.14431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14431)
- Data (counterpoint on quality): LLM dictionary example sentences can match or beat dictionary baselines. A 2024 method reached an 85.1% win rate against Oxford example sentences (prior models: 39.8%) on OxfordEval, a metric that tracks human judgments. — [arXiv 2404.06224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06224). Expert evaluations also rate LLM learner's-dictionary definitions as comparable to human lexicographers, but quality drops for lower-resource languages. — [arXiv 2601.01842](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.01842), [arXiv 2410.03182](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03182v2)

### Inferences
- For EN vocabulary, a common and well-resourced case, LLM card quality is probably good enough. The real failure modes are sense disambiguation (which meaning of a polysemous word) and RU↔EN translation nuance, not factual hallucination. Showing the source context and allowing one-tap edits answers most of the objections.
- "User chooses the word, AI builds the card" keeps the selection step, which is the part learners say they value, and automates the step they complain about.

### Gaps
- No Reddit data (tool access blocked). r/Anki and r/languagelearning are probably the largest single source of complaints. The report writer should flag this.
- I found no systematic error-rate study of LLM vocabulary cards (translation + IPA + examples) for RU→EN in particular.

## 2. Learning science: generation effect, FSRS vs SM-2, SRS dropout

### Takeaway
There is a real benefit to making your own cards (d ≈ 0.45 for memory in Pan et al. 2022). But the measured benefit came even from paraphrasing or copy-paste creation, and the materials were text passages, not vocabulary. FSRS clearly predicts recall better than SM-2: it wins on 99.6% of collections by log loss. The best-documented practical problem with SRS is falling behind on reviews.

### Cited Findings
- Data: Pan, Zung, Imundo, Zhang & Qiu (2022, *Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition*) ran six experiments with a 48-hour delayed test. User-generated flashcards beat premade ones for memory (d = 0.45, 95% CI [0.25, 0.66]) and usually for application questions (d = 0.29). They won in 5 of 6 experiments. The benefit was "especially pronounced" for cards made by paraphrasing or copy-paste, and it held against both high- and low-quality premade cards. The authors acknowledge that premade cards are more convenient and save time. — [Pan et al. 2022 PDF](https://sc-pan.github.io/pdf/PZIZQ_2022.pdf)
- Data: a generation-effect meta-analysis found an overall effect of about d = 0.40 across 86 studies (Bertsch et al., 2007). — [Bertsch et al., Memory & Cognition](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03193441), summarized in [Structural Learning](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/generation-effect-active-learning)
- Data: the open-spaced-repetition SRS benchmark (Anki review logs from about 10k users, ~727M reviews; 349.9M reviews used for evaluation without same-day reviews; checked Oct 2026) reports log loss / RMSE(bins) / AUC as follows. FSRS-6: 0.3460 / 0.0653 / 0.7034. FSRS-7: 0.3401 / 0.0634 / 0.7167. FSRS-5: 0.3561 / 0.0742. FSRS-4.5: 0.3625 / 0.0764. HLR (Duolingo's model): 0.4694 / 0.1275. LSTM, GRU and RWKV neural models score better still; RWKV-Instant reaches log loss 0.2773. — [srs-benchmark README](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark)
- Data: Expertium's benchmark write-up reports that FSRS-6 (with recency weighting) has lower log loss than Anki's SM-2 on 99.6% of user collections. It also warns that a fully fair comparison is impossible because SM-2 doesn't natively predict probabilities. — [Expertium: Benchmark](https://expertium.github.io/Benchmark.html)
- Secondary claim: FSRS needs 20–30% fewer reviews than SM-2 for the same retention. The primary benchmark page doesn't state this, so treat it as unverified. — [Diane.app guide (May 2026)](https://www.diane.app/en/guides/fsrs-vs-sm2), [Migaku blog](https://migaku.com/blog/language-fun/spaced-repetition-in-2026-what-actually-works-for-language-learners)
- Data: In the medical student cohort, 75% "never or rarely kept up with daily reviews." — [PMC12662189](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12662189/)
- Anecdote/marketing: the "review pile" guilt loop (skip a few days → big backlog → quit) and a "review wall" about 2 weeks in are described as the main reasons people abandon Anki. Source is a competitor's blog with no data. — [my-senpai: Why People Quit Anki](https://my-senpai.com/insights/why-people-quit-anki.html)
- Open-source FSRS: fsrs4anki has about 4.1k GitHub stars (Oct 2026), and a Rust port (fsrs-rs) exists, so a standalone app can embed the scheduler. — [fsrs4anki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki), [srs-benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark)

### Inferences
- Since even copy-paste creation produced the benefit, the act of choosing a word and engaging with it briefly may carry much of the effect. A bot where the user picks the word and maybe confirms or edits the generated card probably keeps part of that benefit. This is inferred, not tested for vocabulary.
- An app with its own SRS would not compete on the algorithm, because FSRS is open and already in Anki. Any edge has to come from capture friction, card quality and backlog management.

### Gaps
- I found no study that compares AI-generated vocabulary cards chosen by the learner against vocabulary cards the learner wrote.
- I found no published cohort data on how long general (non-medical) Anki users keep going, such as the share still reviewing after 30 or 90 days. Anki does not publish this.

## 4. Willingness to pay: price points, conversion, indie revenue

### Takeaway
People do pay in this category. Typical prices are $5–15/month or about $45/year for education apps. AnkiMobile shows that a one-time $25 price sells well. An AI add-on (Smart Notes) charges $5–20/month on top of free Anki. Conversion in education is slow and modest: the median Year-1 realized LTV is about $23 per payer, and only 17% of new apps reach $1k MRR within two years. AI apps earn more per payer but keep subscribers worse.

## 5. Retention benchmarks and habit formation in language-learning apps

### Takeaway
Education apps keep few users: roughly 27–29% on Day 1 and 2–8% on Day 30, depending on the source. Duolingo is an outlier, with reported D1 above 55% and D30 around 12%, built on years of streak and notification optimization. An SRS-first app inherits the extra backlog problem, which dominates anecdotal reasons for quitting.

### Cited Findings
- Data (aggregated 2026 benchmarks, mixed provenance): education apps about 27–29% D1 and about 8% D30 (some sources say 2%). The average app loses over 70% of users within 30 days (AppsFlyer 2025). — [EngageLab 2026 benchmarks](https://www.engagelab.com/blog/increase-app-retention), [Adjust retention guide](https://www.adjust.com/resources/guides/user-retention/), [Business of Apps education benchmarks](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/education-app-benchmarks/) (403 on fetch; numbers not verified there)
- Secondary data on Duolingo: D1 retention reportedly above 55%, up from 13% at launch. D30 reportedly 12.0% and D365 6.8%. DAU/MAU ratio about 28.6%, vs roughly 10–15% for typical education apps. These come from marketing blogs and are undated; treat them as indicative. — [Udonis Duolingo stats](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-apps/duolingo), [Propel](https://www.trypropel.ai/resources/duolingo-customer-retention-strategy)
- Data (official): Duolingo says it drove multiple retention measures to all-time highs in Q2 2026. DAU grew 23% YoY to 58.7M. — [Customer Experience Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/duolingos-user-retention-hits-all-time-high-as-app-boosts-stickiness/827499/), [SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)
- Data (education, RevenueCat): education has the lowest share of first-month cancellations (30%). Users who commit tend to stay. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)
- Data (Anki, medical students): 56.6% multitask during Anki (exercising, eating), so short mobile sessions are the norm. 75% rarely keep up with daily reviews. — [PMC12662189](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12662189/)
- Design paper (Jul 2026, no user data): Memdora (FSRS-6, iOS/Android/web/extension) argues that "flip and self-rate" plus creating cards away from the reading context are key weaknesses. It proposes one-gesture card creation at the point of reading and effort-based rewards instead of app-presence streaks. — [arXiv 2607.25096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25096)

### Inferences
- Habit loops proven at Duolingo scale (streaks, push notifications) work, but they reward showing up rather than learning. For a capture-first product, the natural habit trigger is the moment you meet a word, which is external and not scheduled. Reviews then need a separate trigger such as notifications or a widget.
- A Telegram bot already provides a push channel. Daily review prompts inside Telegram may be a cheaper retention lever than a native app. This is untested.

### Gaps
- No retention data for SRS-specific apps (AnkiDroid, Noji, Mochi) was found.
- I found no controlled evidence on how streaks or notifications affect long-term vocabulary retention, as opposed to engagement.

## 6. Capture channels learners actually use

### Takeaway
The biggest observable capture channels are browser and media extensions (Language Reactor has about 2M Chrome users) and reader pop-up dictionaries (Yomitan). Kindle Vocabulary Builder exports and video/game miners serve the more hardcore immersion crowd. Messenger bots are a minor channel, and I found no usage data for them.

### Cited Findings
- Data: Language Reactor (Netflix/YouTube dual subtitles with word saving) shows 2,000,000 users on the Chrome Web Store (fetched Oct 2026). Yomitan (pop-up dictionary that sends to Anki) shows 100,000 Chrome users and about 2.9k GitHub stars. — [Chrome Web Store: Language Reactor](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/hoombieeljmmljlkjmnheibnpciblicm), [Chrome Web Store: Yomitan](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/likgccmbimhjbgkjambclfkhldnlhbnn), [Yomitan GitHub](https://github.com/yomidevs/yomitan)
- Data: Kindle Vocabulary Builder → Anki has several open-source and free tools (Fluentcards, Kindle2Anki, vocabulary-to-anki, Maple Anki Utility, and Fluentcards AI, which adds LLM explanations). Vocabulary Builder keeps only about 2,000 words and drops the oldest. — [fluentcards-ai](https://github.com/gustavostz/fluentcards-ai), [Maple Anki Utility](https://github.com/liuzikai/Maple-Anki-Utility), [vocabulary-to-anki](https://npmjs.com/package/vocabulary-to-anki)

### Inferences
- A Telegram bot works for people who are already in Telegram (big in the RU/CIS market), but most capture happens in a browser, video player or e-reader. A public product would probably need a share-sheet/OS text-selection entry point or a browser extension. A capture field that keeps the source sentence would also answer the polysemy complaint.
